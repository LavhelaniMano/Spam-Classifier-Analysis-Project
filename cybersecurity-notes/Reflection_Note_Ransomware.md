# Reflection Note: Cybersecurity Terminology – Ransomware

One cybersecurity term that captured my imagination was **Ransomware**. Although the glossary does not provide a separate definition for ransomware, it defines **malware** as "a program that is inserted into a system, usually covertly, with the intent of compromising the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of the victim's data, applications, or operating system."

I found this terminology particularly interesting because ransomware combines technical skills with psychological pressure. Attackers do not only target computer systems; they also exploit fear, urgency, and the value of sensitive information. It demonstrates that cybersecurity is not just about protecting technology but also about protecting people, businesses, and governments from financial and reputational damage.

A recent cybersecurity incident in South Africa that relates to this terminology occurred in **March 2026**, when **Statistics South Africa (Stats SA)** confirmed that its online Human Resources recruitment database had been compromised during a cyberattack. The attackers reportedly stole hundreds of thousands of files and demanded a ransom, threatening to publish the stolen information if payment was not made. Stats SA stated that the affected system was limited to its online job application platform and that the organisation had begun responding to the incident while notifying the relevant authorities.

This incident shows why ransomware remains one of the most dangerous cybersecurity threats. Even when an attack targets only one system, it can expose personal information, interrupt organisational operations, damage public trust, and require significant resources to investigate and recover from the breach. It also demonstrates the importance of strong cybersecurity practices such as regular backups, multi-factor authentication, continuous monitoring, employee security awareness training, and well-developed incident response plans.

As a Computer Science Honours student with an interest in IoT cybersecurity, this terminology has motivated me to think beyond traditional computer networks. IoT devices often have limited security controls and may become entry points for ransomware attacks on larger systems. This reinforces the importance of designing secure IoT environments with strong authentication, encryption, continuous monitoring, and effective intrusion detection to reduce the likelihood of ransomware compromising connected devices.

Overall, learning about ransomware has made me appreciate how cybersecurity combines technology, risk management, and human behaviour. It has strengthened my interest in researching practical methods to improve cyber resilience and protect organisations from increasingly sophisticated attacks.
